06What metrics would you use to determine whether our marketing is working?

In priority order, and deliberately not starting where most people start:

  • Leads to booked appointments. The one almost nobody measures, and the only one that pays rent. Every other marketer you talk to will report leads. Leads are not revenue; booked, kept appointments are.
  • Speed to first response. Measured in minutes, not hours. This is the single biggest lever on the number above, and it's the one that improves fastest.
  • Show rate. A booked appointment that doesn't walk in is a lead you paid for twice.
  • New patient to member conversion. What percentage of first-time patients end up in a membership. This is your business-model metric — it's the difference between a practice and a brand.
  • Membership retention, in months. Recurring revenue is only recurring if people stay.
  • Revenue per patient, and revenue per provider. Per-provider is how you'll know the RN launch worked, and how you'll know when to hire the third one.
  • Review velocity. New reviews per month. This compounds into everything else — it makes your advertising cheaper later, which is a real financial reason to do it first.
  • Cost per acquired patient, and return on ad spend. Last on purpose. These only become meaningful once there's advertising to measure, and once everything above is working — otherwise you're optimizing the cost of pouring water into a leaking bucket.

You'll get these monthly, in plain language, with what changed and what I'm doing about it. Not a dashboard you have to interpret.
